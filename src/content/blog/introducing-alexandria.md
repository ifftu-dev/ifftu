A decentralized knowledge preservation system built to outlast any institution. The library that never burns is now under active development.

The Library of Alexandria was one of the ancient world's greatest achievements — and its destruction one of history's greatest losses. For centuries, a single fire could erase the collective knowledge of civilizations.

Today, the threat is different but the risk is the same. Knowledge isn't stored in scrolls anymore — it's stored on servers owned by corporations, governed by terms of service, and subject to the whims of the market and political pressure.

## WHAT IS ALEXANDRIA?

Alexandria is a decentralized, peer-to-peer knowledge preservation system. No single server. No single owner. No single point of failure. Documents are cryptographically verified, distributed across a global network, and accessible to anyone.

Think of it as a library that exists everywhere and nowhere. A library that can't be burned because there is no single building to set fire to. Every node is a backup. Every participant is a librarian.

## CORE PRINCIPLES

**Censorship resistance.** No government, corporation, or individual can remove content from Alexandria unilaterally. The network decides, not a boardroom.

**Cryptographic integrity.** Every document is hash-verified. You know it hasn't been tampered with. The truth doesn't need permissions.

**Offline-first.** Alexandria works without an internet connection. Because access to knowledge shouldn't depend on access to infrastructure.

## CURRENT STATUS

Alexandria is in active development. We're building the core protocol, designing the peer discovery mechanism, and prototyping the content verification system. The code is open source and available on GitHub.

We'll be publishing regular development updates here. If you want to contribute, check out the repository or reach out through our contact page.
